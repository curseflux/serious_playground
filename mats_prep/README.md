## Suggested schedule

Roughly two weeks out, ~8 hours total. Compress proportionally if you have less.

**Session 1 (~45 min).** Read the actual reasoning transparency page at
<https://coefficientgiving.org/research/reasoning-transparency/>, including the GiveWell
example in footnote 1. Then read `01_playbook.md`. Correct anything in
`02_reasoning_transparency.md` that turns out to be wrong — I couldn't verify it.

**Session 2 (~45 min).** Read `03_hypothesis_library.md` properly. Then close it and try
to reproduce the Tier 0 list from memory. You want these available without lookup.

**Session 3 (~2 hrs).** Scenario A under real conditions: 100-minute timer, no AI, no
interruptions, phone away. Then grade against `06_rubric.md` while it's fresh.

**Session 4 (~1 hr).** Get the answer critiqued (bring it back to me, or a friend who
does research). Write down your three specific weaknesses.

**Session 5 (~2 hrs).** Scenario B, timed. Focus on the three weaknesses. Re-grade.

**Session 6 (~30 min), day before.** Re-read `01_playbook.md` §3 (the six moves) and §4
(the clock plan) and the rubric's quick self-review pass. Nothing new.

## The three things that matter most

1. **Get a complete rough answer into every section by minute ~32.** MATS said this
   almost verbatim. It's the highest-value tactical instruction in the brief.
2. **Rank things and say what you're dropping.** The tradeoff is deliberate; dissolving
   it fails the test.
3. **Make a recommendation and attach confidence to it.**

## The warning

The failure mode of preparing for this is arriving with a rigid template and forcing the
scenario into it. Graders can spot a pre-baked answer, and pattern-matching a novel
situation onto a memorized shape demonstrates exactly the absence of research taste this
test is trying to detect. Internalize the *moves*; discard the *format* the moment the
scenario wants something else.
