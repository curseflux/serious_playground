# Self-grading rubric

**Caveat:** this is my reconstruction of what a grader would plausibly weight, derived
from the assessment description. It is not the MATS rubric — I have no access to that.
Use it to find weak spots in a practice answer, not as a score you should believe.

Score each dimension 0–4. Anything at 2 or below is where your prep time should go.

---

### A. Evidence handling (weight: high)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 4 | Every non-trivial fact in the packet is accounted for — used, or explicitly set aside with a reason. Conflicts between sources are named and explained. Missing data is identified and requested. Observation, inference and assumption are visibly distinguished. |
| 2 | Uses the headline numbers, ignores several scattered details, doesn't notice a conflict. |
| 0 | Restates the scenario. |

**Check yourself:** list every distinct fact in the packet. What fraction appears
anywhere in your answer? If a fact is absent, did you decide it was irrelevant, or did
you not notice it?

### B. Hypothesis quality (weight: high)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 4 | 3–5 hypotheses, each precise enough to be falsifiable. Mundane/instrumentation causes included. At least one genuinely alarming possibility taken seriously rather than name-checked. Non-exclusivity noted where relevant. Each has *disconfirming* evidence listed, not only supporting. |
| 2 | Two or three hypotheses, vaguely stated, only supporting evidence given. |
| 0 | One hypothesis, asserted. Or a list of safety vocabulary with no content. |

**Check yourself:** for your leading hypothesis, did you write down the strongest thing
that argues against it? If not, that's a free point you left on the table.

### C. Prioritization under constraint (weight: high — likely the differentiator)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 4 | Investigations explicitly ranked, with a stated criterion. Costs engaged against the actual budget given. Says what is being dropped and what that costs. Cheap confound-killers come first. Sequencing is realistic ("first hour / first day / rest of week"). |
| 2 | A list of good ideas, unordered, or ordered without justification. |
| 0 | "I would investigate all of these." |

**Check yourself:** if your plan were executed with half the stated budget, does your
answer say what survives?

### D. Experiment design / discrimination (weight: medium-high)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 4 | Each proposed test is tied to specific hypotheses and would produce different results under each. States in advance what outcome would update you and by roughly how much. Includes the unglamorous high-value moves (read the transcripts; re-run the baseline on the new setup; ask for confidence intervals). |
| 2 | Sensible tests, but not clearly tied to hypotheses; "this would tell us more." |
| 0 | Tests that can't distinguish anything, or that require resources you were told you don't have. |

### E. Decision-relevance (weight: high)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 4 | Makes an actual recommendation. Considers a real option set beyond ship/hold — mitigations, staged rollout, capability gating, monitoring with a rollback trigger. Names costs on *both* sides honestly. Pre-commits to an escalation threshold. Scopes to their own authority. Catches any decision deadline earlier than the headline one. |
| 2 | Recommends something, but the option set is binary and the tradeoff is asserted rather than analysed. |
| 0 | Analysis with no recommendation. Or announces decisions well above their pay grade. |

### F. Reasoning transparency & calibration (weight: high)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 4 | Confidence attached to major claims using one consistent, defined scale. Assumptions surfaced with their consequences ("if this is wrong, my conclusion flips"). "What would change my mind" stated per-claim. Load-bearing considerations flagged. Scope note on what wasn't considered. |
| 2 | Some hedging language, no scale, uncertainty mentioned once at the end. |
| 0 | Confident assertions throughout, or fake precision with invented percentages. |

### G. Communication (weight: medium)

| Score | Looks like |
|---|---|
| 4 | Summary-first and the summary alone is actionable. Scannable structure, tables where tables help. No padding, no scenario restatement. Body is consistent with the summary. |
| 2 | Readable but front-loaded with context; the point arrives late. |
| 0 | Wall of text, or the summary contradicts the body because it was written first and never revised. |

### H. Completeness (weight: pass/fail, effectively)

Did you fill in **every** required section at a reasonable standard? MATS told you
explicitly to prioritize this. An excellent answer with an empty section 5 will
probably score below a solid answer with all six filled.

---

## Quick self-review pass (do this in the last 8 minutes of any practice run)

1. Can someone read only my summary and act correctly? Does it match the body?
2. Is there a number or a rank anywhere in my prioritization section?
3. Did I write down disconfirming evidence for my own leading hypothesis?
4. Did I say what would change my mind, specifically?
5. Did I make a recommendation, and is it scoped to my role?
6. Is every required section non-empty?
7. Is there any fact from the packet I never mentioned? Should I have?
8. Did I name a cost on the side I'm recommending? (If everything favours my
   recommendation, I'm probably not being honest.)
