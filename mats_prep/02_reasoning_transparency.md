## Claims I'm confident about (corroborated by search snippets today)

- **Definition.** Reasoning transparency prioritizes sharing information about your
  underlying thinking processes, communicated in a way that makes it easier for the
  reader to work out *what updates to make in response*. **[High confidence — this
  phrasing appeared near-verbatim in multiple search results.]**

- **The framing goal.** Give a full and honest picture of what you believe, how
  confidently you believe it, what your reasons are, and why someone might reasonably
  disagree. **[High confidence — appeared close to verbatim.]**

- **The top recommendations.** Search results consistently gave these as the headline
  advice: **[High confidence]**
  1. Open with a linked summary of key takeaways.
  2. Throughout the document, indicate which considerations are most important to your
     key takeaways.
  3. Throughout the document, indicate how confident you are in major claims, and what
     support you have for them.

- **On citations.** Give page numbers and quote the relevant passage directly, so the
  reader doesn't have to go track the source down. **[Moderate-high confidence —
  search corroborated this.]**

- **On confidence.** Claims are always made with some degree of confidence whether or
  not you show it; telling the reader how confident you are helps them see where the
  uncertainty actually lives. **[Moderate-high confidence.]**

## Why MATS points at it

The test is graded on whether a reader can audit your reasoning and calibrate off it.
That is exactly what this document is about. Two practical consequences:

1. **Your answer should be auditable.** For each conclusion, a reader should be able to
   see what fact it rests on, how confident you are, and what would overturn it.
2. **Confidence is a first-class part of the content, not a garnish.** Write "likely
   (~75%), and the main thing that would move me is X" rather than "possibly."

The single sentence to carry into the exam room:

> *Write so that a busy, skeptical reader can figure out how much to update, and in
> which direction, without having to reconstruct your reasoning for you.*
